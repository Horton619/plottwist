Chevron seating — interpretations

Updated after your feedback: the correct interpretation is panel D at the bottom — section stays rectangular, aisle stays straight, but each ROW within an outer section is a continuous angled line of tables anchored at the aisle, rotated as a unit.

A. Rotate around centroid — current

Whole section rotates around its centroid. Aisle stays straight; section's inner edge tilts away from the aisle on one side and toward it on the other.
Chevron tables push INTO the straight aisles at the front and pull AWAY at the back (or vice versa). Inside edges no longer line up with anything.

B. Rotate around front-inner corner

Pivot is the corner of each chevron section closest to the stage AND closest to the venue centerline. The whole section swings outward. Adjacent aisle becomes a TRAPEZOID matching the tilted inner edge.
Inner edge stays anchored at the front. The aisle is now a TRAPEZOID — narrow at the front (matching the original 50" aisle), wider at the back as the chevron section swings out. Matches your reference image.

C. Per-chair rotation only

Chair / table positions stay on the original rectangular grid. Each chair / table gets its rotation set to the chevron angle, but doesn't move. Section is still rectangular, aisle is straight.
Each chair "tips" by 15°. Aisles stay straight. Visually subtle — chairs face slightly inward but the row itself is still horizontal. Doesn't match the reference image.
What I think you want — interpretation B.
Outer chevron sections rotate as rigid units around their FRONT-INNER corner. The aisle adjacent to a chevron section is no longer a uniform-width vertical strip — it's a wedge / trapezoid whose outer edge follows the section's tilted inner edge. The "aisle dictates the shape of the section" because its INNER edge (against the central section) stays a clean straight line; the section's INNER edge flushes against the aisle's tilted OUTER edge.
